Kitchen Water Damage in Vineland

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. For homeowners in Vineland, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Warning Signs

  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Side panels on the base cabinets that are flaking or swollen at the bottom
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. The appliance hides all of it until the floor changes.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Layers of Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Our Process

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. It is common for water beneath a kitchen floor to spread further than you would guess.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Start Swelling

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.
