A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Vineland Restoration provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Vineland, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

What a Professional Brings to Sewage Cleanup

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. People exposed to the pathogens in sewage can become seriously ill with skin and respiratory infections, hepatitis or gastrointestinal disease.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Slow drains throughout the house that plunging does not fix
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Food waste, oils and grease block pipes, so put them in the trash and not the sink
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
