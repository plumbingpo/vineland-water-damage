Dealing With AC Leak Water Damage in Vineland

All day long an AC system wrings humidity from indoor air, and the resulting water needs a way out. Normally it drips off the coil into a pan and leaves through a small drain line. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. Attic and closet air handlers send the spill directly into the surrounding ceiling, walls or flooring.

People tend to underestimate what a small, steady leak can do. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. You will still need an HVAC technician to clear the drain or fix the unit, and we can work around their visit.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the System DownNo more condensate forms once the AC is turned off at the thermostat. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Keep Clear of Wet ElectricalDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Clear Water From the PanIf you can reach the pan safely, soak up or wet-vac the standing water so it stops spilling over. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Dry the Easy SpotsMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsCall us to check how far the water spread, and call an HVAC technician to clear the drain line. Keep the AC off until the repair is made.
Insurance

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. Denial is possible when the adjuster concludes the leak ran for weeks or months, since that reads as a gradual or maintenance problem. Either way, the cost of fixing the AC unit is typically left out. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

Cost

What Affects the Cost

  • If mold is present, the added work of containing and removing it.
  • The water category, because Category 2 needs more cleaning than Category 1.
  • The location of the air handler, since attic, garage and closet leaks spread differently.
  • Rot in the stand beneath the air handler, which can mean rebuilding it.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Coil icing brought on by a filter that was not changed or a refrigerant charge that is low.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • The safety float switch being absent or broken, so the unit kept running.
  • Algae, slime and dust from the coil plugging the condensate drain.
  • A frozen evaporator coil that thawed faster than the pan could drain.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Mold Near the Return

Damp drywall and wood beside the air handler can grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, and the return air can pull spores into the ductwork and through the house.

Ceilings Under the Unit

Overflow from a unit in the attic lands on the ceiling drywall. First comes a stain, next a sag, and after days of soaking a piece may drop into the bedroom or hall underneath.

Closet Walls and Platform

Many closet installations put the air handler on a wood platform, with the return box below it. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Trim and Baseboards

Baseboards and door casings near the closet draw water up from the floor. Fiberboard trim swells and crumbles, so it is usually replaced, not dried.

How We Fix It

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

1

Measure the Spread

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

2

Remove Water and Wet Drywall

We extract the water sitting in the carpet, the platform and the pan. Saturated or moldy insulation and drywall are removed, which opens the cavity to dry air.

3

Dry the Structure

We monitor readings throughout the several days, usually 3 to 5, that the fans and dehumidifiers need. Machines are positioned to leave the HVAC technician a clear path to the unit.

4

Clean and Restore

Affected surfaces get an antimicrobial treatment, because condensate that has sat for days is rarely clean. New flooring, baseboard, drywall and insulation follow, and fresh paint makes the area match.
